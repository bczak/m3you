---
title: Radio button
description: One choice from a set, on its own or in a group.
---

Radio buttons let people pick exactly one option. Use `RadioGroup` with
`RadioGroupItem` so the group manages `name`, selection and arrow-key
navigation; reach for the bare `RadioButton` only when you are wiring up your own
form state.

<Example name="radio-button/group" title="A radio group" layout="stretch" />

Bare, uncontrolled `RadioButton`s that share a `name` behave like a native
radio group: when a click or an arrow key moves the selection, the radio that
lost it redraws itself as unselected.

Inside a `<form>`, a form reset (`form.reset()` or a `<button type="reset">`)
puts an uncontrolled `RadioGroup` back on its `defaultValue`, and a bare
uncontrolled `RadioButton` back on its `defaultChecked`. As with a native reset,
`onValueChange` is not called. A controlled group or radio is left alone: its
selection comes from your `value` or `checked`, so you reset that state yourself.

On both `RadioButton` and `RadioGroupItem`, `className` and `style` apply to the
visible 48dp root (`.md-radio`). Every other prop, including `id`, goes to the
visually hidden native input, so `<label htmlFor>` still points at it.

Name a `RadioGroupItem` with an `aria-label`, an `aria-labelledby`, or a
`<label>` with text that points at its `id` or wraps it. Only when it has none of
these does its `value` become its `aria-label`, so a bare
`<RadioGroupItem value="dark" />` is still announced as "dark". A `<label>` added
after the item mounts takes over the next time the item renders.
