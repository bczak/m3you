The four chip types do different jobs. **Assist** chips trigger an action related
to nearby content. **Filter** chips narrow a set and hold a selected state.
**Input** chips represent a piece of information the user entered, and can be
removed. **Suggestion** chips offer a next step.

`ChipGroup` supplies either a horizontally scrolling row (the default) or a
wrapping layout. Selection remains controlled by each child chip.

A filter chip draws its own check when `selected`, so you never pass one. If you
give a filter chip a `leadingIcon`, it shows while the chip is unselected and the
check takes its place once selected. An input chip with `onClose` renders its own
remove button, and its `trailingIcon` is not shown. The remove button covers the
end of the chip at full height, from the gap after the label to the chip's edge,
so you do not have to hit the 18px icon exactly. Its hover and press feedback and
its focus ring stay on the icon. If the chip is squeezed narrower than its label,
for example a long address in a narrow wrapping group, the label is cut off
before the icon rather than running under it.

## Chip groups

`ChipGroup` lays chips out 8px apart with 8px above and below them, so a single
row of chips is 48px tall. That space also keeps an elevated chip's shadow from
being clipped above and below by the scrolling row. There is no padding at the
sides, so the first chip lines up with the group's start edge. The scrolling row
clips anything that reaches past its start or end, so chips in it draw their
focus ring just inside their edge instead of 2px outside it. That keeps the
whole ring visible on the first chip, on the last chip once you scroll to the
end, and on a chip that is partly scrolled out.

The default `layout="scroll"` keeps the chips on one row that scrolls sideways.
`layout="wrap"` moves chips that do not fit onto further rows, 16px apart.

## Shape morph

Chips keep the baseline M3 8px corners by default. Set `morph` to opt into the
Expressive chip shapes from Jetpack Compose's Material 3 chip tokens: 12px
corners at rest, 8px while pressed, and a full pill once a filter or input chip
is selected. The corners spring between these shapes. With reduced motion, they
change without animating. An input chip with an avatar keeps its pill shape at
rest.
