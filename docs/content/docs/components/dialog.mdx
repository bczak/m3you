Dialogs interrupt to ask for a decision. They are built on Base UI's dialog
primitive, so focus trapping, scroll locking, escape handling and ARIA wiring
come from a library that specialises in them.

Compose a dialog from `Dialog` → `DialogTrigger` → `DialogContent`, with a
`DialogHeader` (holding an optional `DialogIcon` and the `DialogTitle`), then
`DialogDescription` for the supporting text, an optional `DialogBody` for
anything richer, and `DialogFooter` for the actions — all as direct children of
`DialogContent`.

## Long content

The container stops 24px short of the viewport. When its content is taller,
the header and actions stay where they are and the `DialogBody` scrolls; with
no body, the `DialogDescription` scrolls instead. The dialog has no drop
shadow — like every M3 dialog, it sits on its tonal surface over the scrim.

A plain `DialogBody` is a direct child of `DialogContent` and is itself the
dialog's flex item, so `.md-dialog-content > .md-dialog-body` selectors and
`flex`, `flex-shrink` or `min-height` on the body work directly. It has no
minimum height: on a short landscape phone or at high zoom, where the header,
text and actions alone fill the dialog, it can shrink to nothing, so give it a
`min-height` if its content must stay in view.

A bleeding body (see the list dialog below) never shrinks below 112px — two
list rows — unless its list is shorter, so in that same case the list stays
visible and the whole dialog scrolls for the rest: nothing is cut off out of
reach. That floor is held by a layout frame (`.md-dialog-body__frame`) that
`DialogBody bleed` renders around its scroll box; its `ref`, `className`,
`onScroll` and other props all go to the scroll box itself. The floor applies
when the body is a direct child of `DialogContent`; nested in your own
wrapper, the body lays out as a plain one. The frame takes any spare height of
a `DialogContent` you give a fixed height, so a bleeding body styled `flex: 1`
still fills it and the actions stay at the bottom. To have the whole dialog
scroll rather than a bleeding body, wrap the body in your own element instead
of setting `flex-shrink: 0` on it, which would let it spill out of its frame
over the actions.

## List dialog

`DialogBody bleed` runs the body edge to edge, past the container's 24px side
padding, and 24px below the supporting text — the M3 list dialog. A `List`
inside it gets full-width rows, and on a short screen the list is what scrolls.
Lists paint their rows `surface` by default, so set
`--md-list-item-container-color: transparent` on the list to let the rows take
the dialog's surface.

## Dividers

A `DialogDivider` placed directly in `DialogContent` is a full-width rule, edge
to edge, as M3 draws the line between a scrolling body and the actions. Nested
inside a `DialogBody` it stays inset with the body's content.

```tsx
<DialogContent>
  <DialogHeader>…</DialogHeader>
  <DialogDescription>…</DialogDescription>
  <DialogDivider />
  <DialogBody>…</DialogBody>
  <DialogDivider />
  <DialogFooter>…</DialogFooter>
</DialogContent>
```

## Rendering inside a container

`DialogContent` accepts `portalProps`, so you can portal the dialog into a
specific element rather than `document.body` — this site's demo apps use it to
keep dialogs inside the phone frame.

```tsx
<DialogContent portalProps={{ container: myElement }}>…</DialogContent>
```
